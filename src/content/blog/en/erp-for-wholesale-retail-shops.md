Many wholesale and retail shops still run on notebooks, handwritten bills, and one Excel file that only one person knows how to edit. That works until the shop grows or opens a second branch. This article summarizes what a shop management system (ERP) should actually do, based on our experience building systems for wholesale and auto-parts businesses.

## Features that matter

### 1. Stock you can trust, across branches

The heart of any shop is knowing what you have, where, and how many. The system must deduct stock the moment a sale happens, support transfers between branches, and show totals across all branches on one screen. If staff still phone another branch to ask whether an item is in stock, the system has not solved the problem.

### 2. Barcodes and genuinely fast search

At the counter, search speed is everything. The system should find items by name, code, or barcode, and for auto-parts shops it must handle **cross-reference part numbers**, because the same part carries different codes across brands, plus **car fitment**, so staff can answer whether a part fits a specific vehicle.

### 3. Price tiers

Almost every wholesale/retail shop sells at different prices to different customer types: retail, garage or technician, and wholesale. The system should pick the right price automatically based on the customer on the bill instead of relying on staff memory.

### 4. Correct billing and VAT invoices

Receipts, delivery notes, and full VAT tax invoices should come straight from the sales screen, with purchase and sales tax reports ready for the accountant. If the system cannot do this, the shop ends up running two systems in parallel.

### 5. Purchasing with low-stock alerts

The system should tell you what is selling and running low, and generate purchase orders from real data rather than gut feel. A stock-out is lost revenue; overstock is frozen cash.

### 6. Reports that answer the owner's questions

Today's sales, real margin, best sellers, outstanding receivables. These should be visible immediately from a phone, not after month-end closing.

## Features that are usually hype

- **AI sales forecasting.** For shops without a deep history of clean data, forecasts are rarely accurate and rarely used. Collect good data first; add prediction later.
- **Everything in one package.** Systems that try to do everything tend to be hard to use at everything. Pick the one that does your shop's core jobs well.
- **Changing how the whole shop works to fit the software.** Good software adapts to how the counter actually operates, not the other way around.

## How to start

List the three most painful problems in your shop, such as inaccurate stock, slow billing, or unknown real margin. Then look for a system that solves those three first. Trial it with your shop's real data before committing, and ask specifically how existing data (products, customers, outstanding balances) gets migrated.
